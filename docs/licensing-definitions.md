# MG3D Licensing Definitions & Protection Guide

This document outlines the licensing strategy for the MG3D project and provides a guide on how to enforce copyright protection.

## Chosen License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
The MG3D project is licensed under the **Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International License**.

### What this license covers:
1. **Free to Use & Share:** Anyone can download, copy, and redistribute the project files (3D models, code, documentation) in any medium or format.
2. **Attribution (BY):** Users must give appropriate credit to the creator (MG3D), provide a link to the license, and indicate if changes were made.
3. **NonCommercial (NC):** Users **may not** use the material for commercial purposes (e.g., selling printed models, locking the files behind a paywall).
4. **ShareAlike (SA - The "Copyleft" element):** If someone remixes, transforms, or builds upon the material, they must distribute their contributions under the exact same CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license. This prevents others from making proprietary, closed-source versions of your work.

## Copyright Registration and Organizations
* **Registration:** You do **not** need to register your work with any organization. Under international copyright law (the Berne Convention), copyright is automatic the moment the work is created and fixed in a tangible form.
* **Organizations:** Creative Commons provides the legal text for the license, but they do not manage, register, or police your work. You are the sole copyright holder and the one in control of enforcing the license.
* **Activation:** To legally apply the license, simply include a `LICENSE.md` file containing the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 text in the repository, and display a clear statement/badge on the project website.

## How to Protect and Enforce the License
Since there is no "copyright police," enforcing the license is up to you. If you discover someone violating the terms (e.g., selling your models online without permission), follow these steps:

### 1. The Friendly Warning
Often, people violate licenses out of ignorance rather than malice. Sending a polite email or message is the best first step:
> *"Hi, I am the creator of MG3D. This project is licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which strictly forbids commercial use. Please remove this listing immediately, or contact me if you wish to negotiate a commercial license."*

### 2. The DMCA Takedown Notice (Most Effective)
If the violator ignores you, or if they are hosting the content on a major platform (e.g., Etsy, eBay, Shopify, Thingiverse, Printables, GitHub, YouTube), you can file a **DMCA (Digital Millennium Copyright Act) Takedown Notice** directly with the platform.
* Platforms are legally obligated to remove infringing content promptly to maintain their legal protection (safe harbor).
* This process is free and highly effective. Look for the "Copyright Policy", "Report Infringement", or "Report this item" link at the bottom of the respective platform's website.

### 3. Formal Legal Action
If the infringing content is hosted on a private server where the host ignores DMCA notices, the next steps involve formal legal action:
* **Cease and Desist Letter:** Having a lawyer draft and send a formal letter on legal letterhead.
* **Copyright Lawsuit:** Filing a formal lawsuit. This is generally a last resort as it can be time-consuming and expensive, and is usually only pursued if significant money is involved.
